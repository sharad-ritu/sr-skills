# Document shapes

Use the closest row as a coverage check. Adapt the headings and omit sections that do not serve the purpose. For a hybrid document, choose one primary purpose and add only the needed elements from another row.

| Purpose | Lead with | Include as needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R&D | The question, current hypothesis, and what remains unknown. | Existing evidence, constraints, alternatives, experiments, observable success criteria, and the next decision. Separate planned experiments from completed results. |
| Improvement proposal | The proposed change and decision requested. | The current problem and its evidence, expected benefit, alternatives, costs, trade-offs, rollout, and how to judge success. |
| Research | The answer supported by the evidence and its limits. | The question and scope, how evidence was gathered, findings, conflicting evidence, unresolved questions, and implications. |
| Bug list | The affected behavior and issues ordered by impact. | For each issue, a stable ID, environment, reproduction or observation, expected and actual behavior, impact, evidence, and known workaround or status. Distinguish confirmed failures from suspected ones. |
| Task list | The goal and next executable actions. | For each task, the action, completion condition, dependencies, and any known owner, deadline, or blocker. Order by dependencies and priority. |
| Questions for someone | Why their input is needed and what it will unblock. | Grouped, answerable questions. Give each question the context needed to answer and the decision its answer affects. Include neutral options when they reduce effort. |
| Explanation | The main idea and why it matters. | Minimum background, how it works, a concrete example or diagram, limits, and specialist detail where needed. |

Keep evidence and detail beside the statement or item they explain. Use tables when readers need to compare the same fields across items, and short sections when the evidence needs more space.
